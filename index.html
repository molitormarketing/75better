    The list

    Six things, non-negotiable, every single day.

    None of them takes more than fifteen minutes. The difficulty isn't any one of them — it's doing all six on the day you don't want to.

    01

    Wake up with intention

    SAME TIME DAILY · NO SNOOZE · 7 DAYS

    One fixed wake time, weekends included. Not early for its own sake — the same, so your body stops guessing what today is.

    02

    Ten minutes of grounding

    10 MIN · BEFORE THE FIRST SCREEN

    Breath, stillness, sun on your face, bare feet on the floor — whatever grounding means for you. The only condition is that it happens before the phone does.

    03

    100 oz of water

    100 OZ · 3 L · TRACKED

    The one people underestimate. Fill the jug in the morning and finish it before you wind down; it's a logistics problem, not a discipline problem.

    04

    Fiber and protein, three times

    3 MEALS · BOTH IN EACH · NOTHING COUNTED

    Nothing is off the table and nothing gets weighed. Each of your three meals just has to contain both. That's the entire rule, and it's the one that quietly changes your afternoons.

    05

    Fifteen minutes of movement

    15 MIN · ANY KIND · EVERY DAY

    A walk counts. So does stretching on the kitchen floor. Fifteen minutes is small on purpose — small enough that there's no honest excuse on a bad day.

    06

    Ten minutes reading and writing

    10 MIN · PAGE AND PEN · SPLIT AS YOU LIKE

    Read something, then write something — a few unedited lines about the day. Ten minutes covers both, and the writing is the half that does the work.

    The rule that does the work

    Miss anything and the counter goes back to Day 1.

    Not a strike system. Not a grace day. Six for six, or you start the board again tomorrow morning. It sounds harsh until about Day 9, when you notice the internal argument has stopped — because there's no version of the day where skipping one is cheap.

    DAYS 22–45

    The quiet middle

    Motivation is gone and nothing dramatic is happening. The checklist is the only thing carrying you, which is the skill.

    DAYS 46–65

    Payoff

    You wake before the alarm. The 3pm crash stops arriving. This is the part people mean by "20 again."

    DAYS 66–75

    Don't get cute

    More people restart in the last ten days than the first ten. Finish the list exactly as written and don't start improvising.

    Straight answers

    The questions everyone asks on Day 0.

    Do I really go back to Day 1 for one missed item?

    Yes. That's the entire mechanism — the moment there's partial credit, the list becomes a suggestion and your evening starts negotiating. The upside is that the decision disappears: there's nothing to weigh up, you just do the six.

    These all look easy. Where's the catch?

    Each one is easy. Six of them, on the day your flight is delayed and you slept badly, is not. The program isn't testing whether you can do fifteen minutes of movement — it's testing whether you can do it 75 times without a single exception, which is a completely different skill and the only one that transfers.

    What counts as grounding?

    Anything deliberate and unplugged: breathwork, a sit, stretching in silence, standing outside with coffee and no phone. You name yours on Day 0 and keep it for the full 75 so it becomes a place you go rather than a decision you make.

    Is this safe for me?

    Talk to your doctor before you start — 100 oz of water a day and a change in how you eat are real changes, and neither is right for everyone. Bring the six rules to the appointment; they're easy to adjust with a professional and much harder to adjust halfway through.

    What exactly do I get for $14.99?

    Three PDFs, emailed at checkout: 75 journal prompts, 75 fifteen-minute movement sessions, and 75 fiber-and-protein meal ideas. Two hundred and twenty-five entries, one set for each day. The tracker is free and always was — you are buying the content, not the checkbox.

    Can I look at the board before I buy?

    Yes — it's open, and you can tick a day on it right now without paying anyone. The guides are the part you're buying: "read something and write something" is easy to say and hard to do at 9pm on Day 40 in front of a blank page, and that's the problem 225 written entries solve.

    Who is this not for?

    Anyone who'd use a strict daily rule to punish themselves rather than build something. If a missed day would send you somewhere dark, this format is the wrong tool and a gentler habit program will get you further.

    Most people restart, don't they?

    Most people restart at least once, and finishers restart an average of twice. It isn't failure — it's the cost of a standard that means something. What separates finishers is what they do the next morning — which is why the journal guide has a prompt written for exactly that morning.

    What happens on Day 76?

    You keep the two or three items that changed your life and drop the scaffolding. The last week of the program is spent deciding which ones those are, so you don't wake up on Day 76 with nothing to do.

    One honest note. This is built for consistency, not for punishment. The rules are strict so you stop deliberating — not so you can prove something to yourself by suffering. If it ever starts feeling like the second thing, put the board down. It'll still be there when it's the first thing again.
